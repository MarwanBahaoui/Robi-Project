Steal a Ride

Game loop and first prototype

Updated 4 November 2026  |  Starting theme: vans  |  Incremental collection game

Steal vans from guarded NPC lots and bring them home. Keep a van for passive income or sell it to an NPC trader for immediate cash. Every newly secured type advances a permanent collection of eight discoveries.

STEAL  >  ESCAPE  >  EARN  >  UPGRADE  >  REPEAT

The game loop

Step: 1  Choose
Player action: Compare income, sale value and security.
Result: Choose an earner, a sale or a discovery.
Example: Target a missing collection type.

Step: 2  Approach
Player action: Walk freely to the van and its Steal prompt.
Result: The guard stays stationary until you steal.
Example: No waiting for a patrol.

Step: 3  Steal
Player action: Press Steal and enter in a brief animation.
Result: Alarm sounds; guard reacts before chasing.
Example: Gain control and a short head start.

Step: 4  Escape
Player action: Follow a simple road back to the garage.
Result: The guard stops chasing at the boundary.
Example: First ride has one obvious route.

Step: 5  Secure
Player action: Bring the stolen van across the safe zone.
Result: Register its discovery permanently.
Example: Collection advances from 0/8 to 1/8.

Step: 6  Earn or sell
Player action: Park it for income or sell it to the trader.
Result: Choose ongoing earnings or immediate cash.
Example: Rusty van earns $1/s if kept.*

Step: 7  Upgrade
Player action: Improve getaway acceleration or buy a slot.
Result: Get moving faster or earn from more vans.
Example: Expand from two slots to three.

Step: 8  Repeat
Player action: Find missing types and attempt better thefts.
Result: Complete eight discoveries to open a new lot.
Example: Keep discoveries even after sales.

* Income values are illustrative. Prices, timers and rewards must be balanced through testing.

The main hook: you escape in the vehicle you just stole. Guards react to the theft; the challenge starts with the getaway. Test that activity before adding a large economy.

Progression and player rules

Guards wait at their posts until a theft triggers an alarm. Give the player vehicle control and a brief, readable head start before pursuit. NPC targets keep the loop playable alone.

Situation: Caught by a guard
Rule: The guard ejects the player with a short, comic ragdoll push onto safe ground. Recover automatically; return the stolen van to its lot. No money, upgrade or secured-van loss.

Situation: After the knockback
Rule: End the pursuit. Give a brief on-foot sprint boost and capture protection so the player can return home or approach another theft. Neither stacks; both end on starting a theft or entering a vehicle.

Situation: Entering the garage
Rule: The guard stops immediately at the marked boundary and returns to its stationary post. Record a discovery only when the stolen van itself crosses into the garage.

Situation: Full garage
Rule: Place the incoming van in one non-earning arrival bay. Sell a parked van to free a slot, or sell the incoming van. Resolve this choice before starting another theft; no automatic discard.

Situation: Selling a van
Rule: Show the trader price, lost income per second and a confirmation. Remove that van and pay once. Its discovery stays recorded. No separate automatic theft cash bonus.

Situation: Duplicate vehicle
Rule: A duplicate gives no new discovery and cannot create a second earning copy of the same type. Sell it to the trader for cash.

Situation: Collecting income
Rule: Parked vans add to a single garage balance. Collect with one interaction. Income continues during thefts in the same session.

Situation: Safe progress
Rule: Only one stolen van can be in transit. Other players cannot steal secured vans in the initial version. Save an unresolved arrival-bay van so reconnecting does not lose it.

Make capture funny and fair

Show a warning before capture. Keep the ragdoll brief and land the player clear of hazards and walls. Restore control as soon as they stand up. The guard returns to its stationary post instead of repeatedly knocking them down. The first theft has generous reaction time and no escape countdown.

Collections and meaningful choices

Eight discoveries in each collection

Each themed collection contains eight distinct van types. A type counts the first time it reaches the garage safely, not when it is merely entered or spotted. Show eight silhouettes and a clear progress counter. Selling a van never removes its discovery; owning eight parking slots is not required.

Finishing 8/8 unlocks the next collection lot. Make all eight types obtainable with current-area equipment and show where missing types can be found. Avoid rare random spawns that leave a player stuck at 7/8. The prototype uses a small test collection; the eight-type structure is validated in a later content test.

Keep for income or sell for cash

Keep valuable earners to improve long-term income. Sell lower earners or duplicates when immediate cash is more useful. The trader replaces discarding and the former automatic theft reward; the player earns sale cash only when a secured van is actually sold. There is no permanent vehicle storage beyond parked vans and one arrival bay.

Show sale prices before a theft and warn when selling the last earning van. Keep an easy starter target available so an empty garage is recoverable. Balance sale cash against passive income and theft duration so neither keeping everything nor repeatedly selling the easiest van always wins.

Two upgrades to start with

Getaway acceleration: a persistent player upgrade that helps each stolen van reach its normal top speed faster. Preserve differences between vehicle types and keep the first theft achievable without upgrades. Garage capacity: start with two earning slots and offer one extra slot in the prototype. Exact upgrade strength and cost need playtesting.

Give each target a different tradeoff

Vehicle: Rusty van
Appeal: Easy starter discovery.
Challenge: Slow guard reaction and a simple road home.

Vehicle: Armored van
Appeal: Higher earning potential.
Challenge: Slower acceleration requires a clean start.

Vehicle: Sport van
Appeal: Fast getaway.
Challenge: A quicker guard reaction after the theft.

Keep roads readable throughout. The first route is short, wide and obvious, without tight turns or branching decisions. Introduce optional shortcuts only after the player understands driving. Vary guard reaction times, vehicle handling and target placement. Never let a guard capture the player before driving controls are available.

Build and test the first version

The first session

Show a starter van with its income, trader value and a clear road home. The player approaches freely and presses Steal. A brief entry animation leads into vehicle control; the alarm and guard reaction signal the chase. At the garage, the guard leaves and the discovery registers. Park the first van to establish income; introduce selling on a later return.

Aim for a first successful theft in roughly one to two minutes, followed shortly by an affordable upgrade. These are design targets to test, not proven benchmarks.

Prototype scope

Build first: World and challenge
Include: One compact map, one garage, one lot, one stationary NPC guard that reacts to theft, and one short, wide road home.

Build first: Vehicles and upgrades
Include: Three van types; two earning slots plus one purchasable slot; one getaway-acceleration upgrade.

Build first: Economy and collection
Include: NPC trader, session income, one arrival bay, permanent discoveries and sale confirmations.

Build first: Readable failure
Include: Guard warnings, a short ragdoll ejection, a temporary sprint boost and capture protection, and a garage boundary that ends pursuit.

Test in two stages: first use three types and a clearly labeled 3/3 test collection to validate stealing, selling and upgrading. Then expand to eight types and test the real 8/8 unlock. Do not show unreachable 8/8 progress in the three-van build.

Before a public playtest, save money, collection and upgrades reliably, and validate rewards and purchases on the server. Local prototypes can use temporary progress.

What to watch in playtests

Observe 5–10 new players. Do they understand that stealing triggers the guard and get time to drive? Can they explain keep versus sell, clear a full garage and retain discoveries after sales? Do they enjoy capture and choose to retry? Check that sprint protection ends on the next theft and that acceleration upgrades feel useful.

Track waiting for cash, repeated farming of one easy target and players stuck on a final missing discovery. Fix confusing objectives and unfair capture before adding content. Small tests find design problems; they do not prove demand.

Keep the scope small

Defer player raids, player-to-player trading, combat, rebirths, offline income, large cities, advanced police chases and monetization. The NPC trader is part of the first version. Expand beyond vans only after the core loop earns repeat play.

Thumbnail direction: silver van $1/s to gold van $9,999/s. These are concept numbers; the final thumbnail must match the actual game. The design has not yet been built or playtested.
